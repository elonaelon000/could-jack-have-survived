## The most important scientific distinctions

- Jack's 18-25 proxy, H2's 18-30 window, and H1/H3's 18-50 window are different predefined groups. Do not compare their percentages as though the age window were fixed.
- H1 and H2 agree with the registered direction in this sample; H3 does not. This does not establish significant or causal effects.
- Uneven missingness across observed groups does not by itself identify the missing-data mechanism as MAR or MNAR.
- First-class men aged 18-25 are only 10 people, with 3 survivors. Widening to 18-30 is H2's registered definition, not an unnoticed substitution.
- No recorded close relatives does not mean alone. `SibSp` and `Parch` exclude friends and many other relationships.
- The alternative-rescue sequence is fiction. The CSV cannot test rescue timing or the physics of floating debris.

## A simple presentation route

Start at the opening question. Give Jack's story in about 20 seconds, then say: "The table cannot tell us whether he fits on the debris. It can tell us who survived."

Show the 10/86 baseline and the three charts. Stop on second class coming last for young men: that is the observation which disrupts the simple class story. In the explorer, switch third to first class while keeping ages 18-25 fixed; show 3/10 rather than presenting 40.9% from the different H2 window as Jack's counterfactual probability.

State which original hypothesis did not survive. Show the missing ages, missing cabins and small comparison cells. Finish with the actual poster, not the fictional sequence. The final line is: "Some people resembling Jack survived. That is a finding; a promise that one change would save him is not."
